# Script and beats: 04 "The Scientist's Insane Time Test"

## 1. Narration (SRT cleaned)

> A scientist dropped people off a tower just to find out what happens to time right before you die. Apparently, when you're terrified, your brain speeds up so much that the whole world slows down. People who survive crashes swear everything went into slow motion. So before they fell, he strapped a little screen to each of their wrists that flashed numbers way too fast to read. If time really slowed down, they'd be able to read them as they were falling. When they landed, every one of them swore the fall took way longer than it really did. But when he asked them what number they saw, not one of them could tell him. You see, when you're frightened, your brain records way more detail than normal. So when you replay it later, it feels like it all happened in slow motion.

(I removed "[music]" from the SRT. This is the real-world David Eagleman free-fall study, retold as a cartoon.)

## 5. Structural template (reusable)

1. Hook = the most extreme literal image of the premise, in one long move (≈5–7 s).
2. Claim → cut *inside the brain* (zoom-through an eye or head) to show the mechanism as a cosy "control room".
3. Real-world evidence shown as a social-comedy vignette with name-tagged extras.
4. Experiment → prop insert (ECU), then a blueprint/diagram for the hypothesis.
5. Result → stat or prop punchline (stopwatch).
6. Twist → visual pun (a "lineup" of numbers).
7. Explanation → back inside the brain (door drawn on the head), the emotional trigger character (Amy / PANIC), and a physical metaphor (more drawings → flipbook).
8. Punchline in an everyday setting with bored friends, then a zoom into the metaphor object that loops back to f1.
